Against infinite scroll

Z. Marsh · 18 min · page 2/9

The first pages of any good essay are a door, not a runway. You are meant to walk through it, sit down, and let it close behind you — a courtesy infinite feeds never extend.

Reading on paper taught my thumbs more about pacing than any app ever did: sheets stack, weight accumulates, and progress becomes something you can feel against your wrist.

“An ending is a promise the body keeps. The stack on your desk is just the receipt.”

Fold that sentence onto your shelf if you like — it’s what paper was for: keeping, not refreshing.
